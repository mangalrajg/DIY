# DIY Shoe Rack — Complete Build Guide

**60 in wide × 48 in tall × 12 in deep** · ¾ in plywood · Removable middle shelves on shelf pins

## What you are building

A 60 in wide × 48 in tall × 12 in deep open shoe rack. Two side panels, a bottom shelf resting on cleats, top board and center divider form a rigid frame — no screw goes through the side panels, so the outside faces stay clean. Six middle-shelf boards (two per level, one in each bay) rest on metal shelf pins, so you can lift them out or change their height any time. A 2 in backstop strip on each shelf keeps shoes off your wall; a front lip keeps them from sliding off.

**Design principles:** wooden cleats throughout (no visible metal brackets), no screws through outside faces, no blind screws (every screw goes into a flat face you can see), every joint positively tied so nothing comes off when you lift the rack.

## Shopping List

### Wood
- 2 sheets of 4×8 ft, ¾ in plywood (sanded pine or BCX)
- 4 pieces of 1×2 × 8 ft pine board

### Hardware
- #8 × 1-¼ in wood screws — one box of 100 (about 70 needed)
- #8 × 2 in wood screws — one small box (2 needed: divider cleats to shelf)
- ¼ in shelf pins — 2 packs (you need 24)
- Wood glue — 1 bottle
- Wood filler (small tub)
- 120-grit sandpaper (a few sheets)
- Finish of your choice: polyurethane or paint (optional)

### Tools
- Drill + drill/driver bits
- Countersink bit, or a 7/64 in bit for pilot holes
- ¼ in drill bit (for the shelf-pin holes)
- Tape measure, pencil, carpenter square
- 2 clamps (strongly recommended for assembly)
- Painter's tape (to mark drill depth)
- Safety glasses
- Hand saw (for small cuts at home: 8 in divider cleats, backstop strips)

## Drilling

Drill ALL holes before assembling. Two kinds of holes:

### Shelf-pin holes (¼ in diameter)
- **Side panels:** ⅜ in deep, drilled on the INSIDE face only (never through)
- **Divider:** 5/16 in deep per side, drilled on BOTH faces (leaves a ⅛ in web in the middle)
- **Pattern:** columns at 2 in and 10 in from the front edge; rows every 2 in from 6 in to 42 in above the floor
- **Critical:** pin holes must be at the exact same height on every panel or shelves will wobble

### Screw pilot holes
- Pilot + countersink for every screw (prevents plywood splitting)
- Use painter's tape on the bit as a depth stop

## Assembly

### Step 1 — Sand and drill everything
- Sand all cut edges with 120-grit sandpaper.
- Drill every hole using the drilling diagrams. Use painter's tape on the ¼ in bit as a depth stop: ⅜ in for side panels, 5/16 in for the divider (both faces).
- Drill pin holes BEFORE assembling — you cannot reach the inside faces afterwards.

### Step 2 — Assemble the rigid frame
1. Lay one side panel flat, inside face up. Run wood glue on the inside face along the bottom 1.5 in.
2. Press a cleat (1×2 × 12 in, on edge) against the glue with its bottom flush with the panel's bottom edge. Drive 3 screws through the cleat into the panel face.
3. Glue and screw a second cleat near the top with its top **flush** with the panel's top edge. Repeat both cleats on the second panel.
4. Stand both panels upright, inside faces toward each other.
5. Lower the bottom shelf onto the two bottom cleats. It should sit flat and level.
6. Mark the divider center (29.25 in from either end of the shelf). Stand the divider on the mark.
7. Glue a divider cleat (1×2 × 8 in, on edge) to each face of the divider. Drive 2 horizontal screws through each cleat into the divider face, then 1 vertical screw (2 in) down through each cleat into the shelf.
8. Set the top board on the top cleats and divider: 4 screws down into the cleats + 2 down into the divider (at 3 in and 9 in from the front, centered at 30 in).
9. Check square: measure both diagonals corner-to-corner. They must match — tap the frame until they do (before glue dries).

### Step 3 — Drop in the middle shelves
1. Push 4 shelf pins into the holes at your chosen height for each board (2 on the side panel, 2 on the divider — same height both sides).
2. Lower each of the 6 boards onto its 4 pins — two boards per level, one in each bay. No screws.
3. To remove: lift off. To change heights: move pins to a different row.

### Step 4 — Backstop strips and front lips
1. **Backstops:** stand a 28.5 × 2 in strip upright (2 in tall) at the BACK edge of each shelf. Middle boards: flip over, drive 3 screws UP from underneath. Bottom shelf: drive 3 screws DOWN through the strip (can't reach underneath).
2. **Front lips:** glue along the front edge, press the 1×2 on, drive 3 screws horizontally through the lip into the shelf edge.
3. Screw positions: ¼, ½, ¾ along the 28.5 in strip length.
4. The strips also stiffen the rack against side-to-side wobble.

### Step 5 — Finish and place
- Fill all screw holes with wood filler. Let dry, sand smooth.
- Wipe off dust, apply 2 coats of polyurethane or paint.
- Place about 1 in off the wall. If tippy, add an L-bracket from the top board to a wall stud.
